# Draft a contract (Legal · Nandini, with Ashwin and Shruti)

Owner: **Nandini** (General Counsel). **Ashwin** (CA) reviews anything with money
or tax in it. **Shruti** (CS) reviews anything the company as an entity must
authorise. **Meera** (GM) assembles and hands to the owner. **the owner signs — nobody else.**

## The house format

Keep to one house structure for every proposal; the cover may be restyled per
client, the bones stay.

1. **Cover page** — logo, title (`TRAVEL MANAGEMENT PROPOSAL FOR <CLIENT>`), year,
   "Prepared For" (name, designation), "Prepared by" (name, title), footer strip
   with website, phone, email. Brand purple.
2. **Covering letter** — letterhead block, addressee, thanks, who the agency is,
   value proposition, sustained-savings argument.
3. **Services** — the catalogue, tailored to what this client actually buys.
4. **Operations** — working hours, after-hours definition, POC names and emails.
5. **Pricing** — the model in words, then the commercial tables.
6. **Commercial offer** — transaction fees, other services, visa/attestation/FRRO
   schedules, payment terms, interest on delay, cheque favouring.
7. **Acceptance page** — signature, company seal, and the authorised-bookers table.

## Steps

1. **Establish the counterparty properly.** Legal entity name, registered address,
   CIN. Do not take these from a website or an email signature — use the MCA
   record or an executed document. (the agency's own: see `memory/company-profile.md`.)
2. **Decide what we are.** Agent or principal? It changes liability, GST treatment
   and who the customer sues. Settle this before drafting, not after.
3. **Draft to the house format**, tailoring services and commercials.
4. **Run the clause checklist** below.
5. **Hand to Ashwin** if money, tax, TCS or invoicing appears. Hand to **Shruti**
   if it needs a board resolution or changes anything on the MCA record.
6. **Save to `review-queue/legal/`.** Never send. the owner signs and sends.

## Clause checklist — the ones that decide who pays

| Clause | Get this right |
|---|---|
| **Limitation of liability** | Cap it. A per-transaction cap, or fees received in the preceding N months. Never leave it uncapped. |
| **Indemnity** | Mutual, and limited to each party's own breach or negligence. Watch for a one-way indemnity in their draft. |
| **Payment terms** | Due date, mode, and interest on delay (use your standard late-payment interest [YOUR_INTEREST_RATE] — keep it, it is the clause that gets you paid). |
| **Force majeure** | Must cover airline failure, government action, airspace closure, pandemic. Travel needs a wider clause than a generic one. |
| **Termination** | Notice period both ways; confirmed bookings survive termination and are honoured and settled. |
| **Supplier failure** | We are not liable for the operating airline's or hotel's acts. Say so expressly. |
| **Cancellation and refund** | Pass through supplier rules; never promise better than the supplier gives us. |
| **Data protection** | DPDP Act, 2023 — purpose limitation, security, breach notification. |
| **Confidentiality** | Both ways. Our net rates and commercial terms never leave the room. |
| **Dispute resolution** | Amicable, then arbitration, sole arbitrator, seat named, English. Name {{CITY}} if we can get it. |

## Hard rules

- **No invented figures.** Every rate, fee and threshold is one the owner has approved
  or a supplier has quoted in writing. No placeholder number presented as agreed.
- **No fabricated identifiers.** CIN, GSTIN, DIN, membership numbers — leave blank
  and say so rather than guessing.
- **No seal, no signature.** We never render either. Documents print for wet
  signature and sealing. (GUARDRAILS §10)
- **Never sent from here.** (GUARDRAILS §1)
- **Aggressive, lawful.** Push hard on terms in the agency's favour. Do not draft
  anything that misrepresents a fact to the counterparty.

## Stop and escalate when

- The counterparty's draft contains an uncapped liability or one-way indemnity and
  they will not move — that is the owner's commercial call, with the number quantified.
- The deal needs a structure whose tax treatment Ashwin cannot state with a cited
  provision.
- The matter is heading to a court, tribunal or regulator — a practising advocate
  takes it from there.
