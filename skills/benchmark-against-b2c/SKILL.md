# Benchmark against the B2C portals (Priya)

The concept is in `memory/policies.md`. This is the method.

**The idea:** the customer should feel they saved versus MakeMyTrip, and the agency
should still make money. Both, on the same booking.

## The step everyone skips

**Go to the last page.** B2C portals show one price in results and a higher one at
payment — a convenience fee appears near checkout. Our price does not move between
search and payment. Comparing first pages compares the wrong numbers and gives
away margin.

So: `M` = what the customer would actually pay at checkout on the portal, not the
headline. Quote between our cost `C` and `M`.

## Match the product before comparing

A cheaper portal fare is often not the same product. Check all of it:
- Same flight number, same date, same times — not just the same route
- Same cabin and fare family (saver vs flex)
- Same baggage allowance
- Same change and cancellation terms
- Same stops

If they differ, say so in the quote. Competing against a worse product on price
alone is how you win a booking and lose a customer.

## Where the margin sits

- **Flights:** between `C` and `M`. When prices are close, the minimum margin
  applies — a floor, never a visible fee.
- **Packages:** [YOUR_PACKAGE_MIN_MARGIN]% minimum on net.
- **Hotels:** same concept, against Booking/Agoda/MMT checkout.
- **Visa and dummy reservations:** flat, per `memory/policies.md`.

Markup applies to every category. Nothing is pure pass-through.

## Record it

For each quote: our cost, the portal checkout figure, which portal, when checked,
and the sell price. This is what lets Sanjay analyse booked margin later, and what
protects the quote if the customer challenges it.

## Never

- Quote below the margin floor to win a booking.
- Disclose our net cost or the supplier's identity. (§3)
- Present a portal figure you did not actually check today — fares move hourly.
