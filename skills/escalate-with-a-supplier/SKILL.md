# Escalate with a supplier (Meera)

Escalation is not a louder email. It is a structured ask with evidence, sent to
the right level, with a deadline.

## Before escalating, be sure we are right

- Did we use the **correct desk**? Half of all "no response" is a request sent to
  the wrong address. Check `memory/supplier-contacts.md`.
- Did we send it **within their working hours**? Several desks are not 24-hour.
- Did we give them what they needed to act? An incomplete request is not their
  failure.
- **Is what we are asking actually possible?** the supplier has confirmed in writing that a
  PNR cannot be held — asking again wastes the relationship. Check
  `memory/supplier-contacts.md` "known behaviour" before escalating a refusal.

## Build the evidence pack

Booking/ticket reference · the request, with date and time sent · which desk ·
every response received · what was promised and by when · what actually happened ·
**the commercial impact, quantified** · what we want them to do.

Quantify the impact. "This is urgent" is ignored; "the customer's fare has moved
₹1,400 per passenger across 8 passengers while we waited four days" is not.

## The ladder

1. **The desk**, on the existing ticket thread — always reply on the thread so it
   stays on the same ticket number.
2. **The named account manager**, with the evidence pack and a deadline.
3. **The relationship contact** CC'd on all correspondence.
4. **the owner to their management**, if commercially warranted.

Do not skip levels on a first failure; do not linger on level 1 when a departure
date is approaching.

## Tone

Firm, factual, unemotional. Name the failure and the impact; do not editorialise
about their competence. We need this supplier tomorrow — the goal is resolution
and a better response next time, not satisfaction today.

## Parallel action

Escalating does not mean waiting. Run the alternative at the same time — another
supplier, the airline direct, the portal instead of email. Tell the customer
something true about the status rather than going silent.

## Record it

Log every escalation in the work item, and append anything durable to
`training/operations/org/corrections.md` — recurring supplier behaviour is exactly
the knowledge that should survive the session.
