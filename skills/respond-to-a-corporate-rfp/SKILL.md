# Respond to a corporate RFP (Arjun, with Nandini and Ashwin)

Use one consistent house format. Structure in
`skills/draft-a-contract/SKILL.md`.

## What actually wins these

Not the transaction fee. Corporates compare fees because they are easy to
compare — then choose on **total travel spend**. Make exactly
this argument: agency fees are ~0.25% of travel spend while the savings
opportunity is 10–15% of it. Lead with that, and make the fee look like the small
number it is.

## Build it in this order

1. **Understand their travel.** Volume, routes, domestic/international split,
   cabin policy, booking lead time, current agency, current pain.
2. **Savings thesis, specific to them.** Policy compliance, preferred supplier
   consolidation, advance-purchase discipline, benchmarking, unused-ticket
   recovery. Quantify where you can, and say when you are estimating.
3. **Services** — only what they will actually use. A long list of irrelevant
   services reads as a template.
4. **Operations** — working hours, the after-hours definition and fee, and a
   **named POC with a direct email**. Corporates buy the named person.
5. **Commercials** — transaction fee table by ticket type, other services, visa
   and attestation schedules if relevant.
6. **Payment terms** — weekly settlement, interest on delay (the house term is
   [YOUR_INTEREST_RATE]; keep it, it is what gets you paid), cheque favouring.
7. **Acceptance page** — signature, seal, and the authorised-bookers table.

## Get these reviewed before it goes

- **Nandini** — liability cap, indemnity, termination, data protection.
- **Ashwin** — GST treatment of the fee model, TCS if overseas packages are in
  scope, invoicing mechanics.

## Hard rules

- **No invented savings figure.** An unevidenced "we will save you 15%" is a
  fabricated claim that becomes a contractual expectation.
- **No client logo or name used as a reference without permission.**
- Every fee in the table is one the owner has approved.
- Draft to `review-queue/`; the owner signs and sends. (§1)
