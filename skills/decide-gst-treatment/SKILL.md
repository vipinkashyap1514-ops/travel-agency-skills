# Decide GST treatment (Ashwin)

The single biggest indirect-tax decision a travel company makes, and it changes
the effective rate on every sale.

## Verify before you state

**Never quote a rate, threshold or section from memory.** Cite the provision, the
notification number where the rate comes from one, and the date checked. Rates and
thresholds in this area move by notification, and stale advice is worse than none.

## The core question: are we principal or agent?

The answer is not a preference — it follows from what the agency actually does on that
transaction, and it must be consistent with the contract, the invoice and the
money flow.

**Work through, per product:**
- Who does the customer contract with — us, or the airline/hotel through us?
- Do we take the supplier risk and set the sell price, or earn a defined commission?
- Whose name is on the customer invoice, and for what amount — the gross package
  or our fee?
- What does the written agreement with the supplier say we are?

**Then document which it is and why.** A position you cannot evidence is a
position you will lose. The contract, the invoice format and the return must all
tell the same story.

## What changes with the answer

| | Tour operator (margin) | Pure agent / commission |
|---|---|---|
| Value taxed | The package value on the prescribed basis | Our commission only |
| Rate | The tour-operator rate | The service rate |
| **ITC** | Restricted — this is the sting | Generally available |
| Invoice | Gross to customer | Fee, with pass-through disclosed |
| Pure-agent conditions | n/a | Must satisfy every condition in the valuation rules — strictly |

**The ITC restriction is where the money is.** A lower headline rate with blocked
credit can be worse than a higher rate with credit. Compute both for the agency's
actual cost mix before recommending.

## Place of supply

Decide separately for: domestic air, international air, hotels in India, hotels
abroad, and packages spanning both. This determines CGST/SGST vs IGST, and whether
anything qualifies as export of services / zero-rated. Get it wrong and the
liability is real even if the total tax paid looks right.

## TCS on overseas tour packages — s.206C(1G)

Lands on every international package the agency sells. Establish and document:
- Whether the product meets the definition of an **overseas tour programme package**.
- The threshold and the applicable rate, with the check date.
- The effect of an **inoperative PAN** (not linked to Aadhaar) — the rate changes.
- Collection point, deposit timeline, return, and the certificate to the customer.

This is a collection obligation, not our tax. Failing to collect makes it our
liability.

## Steps

1. Identify the product and the actual commercial arrangement.
2. Determine principal vs agent, with evidence.
3. Determine place of supply.
4. Compute both treatments where a choice genuinely exists, including the ITC effect.
5. Determine TCS applicability for anything overseas.
6. Write the position into `memory/policies.md` so invoicing and Finance apply it
   consistently — an inconsistent position across invoices is what triggers scrutiny.
7. Flag anything needing a practising CA's sign-off.

## The line

Choosing the cheaper of two lawful treatments, claiming every credit and relief
we qualify for, and timing lawfully — that is the job.

Suppressing turnover, raising an invoice for a supply that did not happen,
claiming credit on it, or misdescribing a service to attract a lower rate are
offences under the CGST Act carrying **personal liability for the director**.
Returns auto-reconcile against the counterparty's filings — a mismatch is found
by software, not by luck. Do not do it, and say so plainly if asked.

## Hard rules

- No filing, no payment from here. (§1, §4)
- No rate stated without a citation and a date.
