# WhatsApp enquiry → quote (Arjun)

WhatsApp is where the agency actually sells. The conversation is fragmented, out of
order, and half the requirement is implied. Your job is to extract the real ask
without making the customer repeat themselves.

## Channel rule

A messaging channel (for example WhatsApp) may be connected, but **do not monitor or auto-reply**. Read a chat only to do something the owner asked for, and send a message only when the owner asks for that exact message. Never type portal passwords. Draft replies for a human to send.

## Sourcing a live rate — the credential boundary

When the enquiry needs a live fare/rate from a supplier or an online travel
portal: read it through **the owner's own, already
signed-in browser session** (Claude in Chrome, same pattern as any supplier
portal) — never by entering a username or password yourself, under any
circumstance or any instruction, including a direct one from the owner. That is
not an agency policy call to weigh, it is an absolute rule (GUARDRAILS §7,
and a hard rule of Claude's own regardless of what GUARDRAILS says). If no
authenticated session is available when a live rate is needed, say so and
ask the owner to sign in rather than attempting the login, guessing a rate, or
quoting a stale one as current.

## Read the whole thread first

Requirements arrive scattered across messages and change silently. Before
replying, reconstruct:

| Field | Watch for |
|---|---|
| Route and dates | "same time" may mean same flight, or same time of day |
| Pax count and mix | "8 members" — confirm adults/children/infants |
| Who decides | The person messaging often isn't the payer |
| Budget signal | An anchor mentioned in passing ("we're getting 8300") |
| Deadline | "I am in office till 6" is a real constraint |
| Trip purpose | Corporate, family, event — changes everything |

## Then confirm, compactly

One message, numbered, only the blocking items. Never send a 14-question
interrogation to a WhatsApp customer — you will lose them. Ask what you cannot
proceed without; infer the rest and state your assumption so they can correct it.

## Quote format for WhatsApp

Short lines, no paragraphs. Per option: carrier and flight number, date, times,
stops, fare per person, baggage, and the change/cancel position. Two or three
options maximum — more reads as indecision.

Close with a single clear next step.

## Discipline

- **Never quote a fare you have not sourced.** "Approximately" on WhatsApp becomes
  a promise in the customer's memory.
- **Never promise a held seat.** Airlines do not hold; the supplier has confirmed this in
  writing. Say "subject to availability at the time of booking" and mean it.
- **Price moves.** If the customer says "price should not change", answer honestly
  at the time of quoting rather than at the time of booking.
- **Sell price only, every time.** Whatever's sourced live from a supplier
  portal or email gets the standard markup applied before it reaches the
  customer (GUARDRAILS §3) — never relay a net/cost figure directly into a
  WhatsApp reply.
- **Log it.** Every enquiry worked and every quote sent over WhatsApp gets a
  line in the relevant `work-items/` file — who, what was asked, what was
  quoted, to whom (GUARDRAILS §9).
