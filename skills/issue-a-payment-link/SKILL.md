# Issue a payment link (Deepak)

## Prepare, never transact

This playbook **prepares** the request. Generating the link in the gateway,
sending it, and accepting the money are human actions. No employee moves money.
(GUARDRAILS §4, §1)

## What the request must carry

Customer name · booking or quote reference · **exact amount and currency** ·
what it is for, itemised · whether it is a deposit or full payment · validity ·
and, for a deposit, **whether it is refundable and by when**.

An unlabelled amount produces a dispute later. Itemise.

## Gateway position

Know each gateway's status and any restriction in its onboarding undertaking (for example, a gateway limited to certain services) before routing a collection through it. Keep this in your own notes.

Settlements pause on bank holidays — factor this into cash-flow promises.

## Fraud and chargeback discipline

Card-not-present on travel is a high-risk category. Before requesting payment:

- The payer's name should match the lead passenger, or the relationship should be
  explained and recorded.
- Be cautious with: high-value first-time customers, imminent departure dates,
  a third-party card, or a customer resisting any verification.
- For corporate accounts, invoice on agreed terms rather than taking a card.
- **Never request card numbers, CVV or OTP over WhatsApp or email**, and never
  accept them if sent. If a customer sends card details, tell them to stop and
  delete the message.

## After payment

Reconcile receipt against the booking the same day. Match the settlement to the
transaction, not just the total. Flag anything unmatched to the owner immediately —
an unreconciled receipt is how a refund gets paid twice.

## Stop and escalate when

- The amount differs from the quote.
- A refund is requested before the service is delivered.
- Any chargeback is received — evidence pack, then the owner.
